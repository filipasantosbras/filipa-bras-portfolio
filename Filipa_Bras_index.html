Positioning & Messaging

The real challenge of a multi-brand portfolio isn't creative — it's decision architecture. I ran five value propositions, five audiences, and five levels of market maturity at once, with a single team and no shared CRM infrastructure.

That forced me to build explicit prioritization criteria between brands — which request serves the most urgent commercial objective, not the most recent one — and to keep every brand's message distinct even while sharing team resources.

On a B2B international-partnerships brand (Brand Delta), I repositioned LinkedIn communication around social proof and international events instead of generic institutional content — result: +315% LinkedIn lead growth and +213.6% impressions.

This same instinct for positioning started years earlier, in a very different market: six years shaping product-market fit and competitive differentiation for a healthcare brand at Sentido Auditivo.

Principle I apply:

  • One value proposition per brand not shared
  • Priority by commercial impact not urgency
  • Message tuned to decision cycle not to channel

Executive Presentation

In May 2026, I prepared and presented a strategic positioning document for the marketing function to the group board — not an activity report, but a business case.

"Identified gaps vs. solvable gaps"

The document's starting point wasn't defending the team — it was clearly separating what sat within marketing's control from what required group-level decisions and investment (CRM, annual budgeting, governance). That distinction turns "why isn't this done yet" into "what do we need to decide, and when."

I quantified the sustained operational load (10–15 weekly requests from business units, one major project per month, four paid channels managed across five brands) — a data point the group's original analysis hadn't captured.

Document structure

  1. Acknowledge the gaps identified by the external consultancy
  2. Matrix: what depends on the team vs. what depends on the group
  3. Quantify the real operational volume, not the perceived one
  4. Explicit alignment with the group's four transformation priorities
  5. Concrete ask to the board: +2 FTE, annual budget per brand, CRM approval

Thought Leadership

Three original perspectives, written for this portfolio.

Marketing Operations

Marketing without a net: what running five brands without a CRM taught me about priority

Without a CRM, every request feels urgent. The discipline that separated what mattered from what was just noise wasn't a tool — it was one simple test: which of these requests moves revenue this week, and which just looks productive? I learned to say no to well-meaning requests that served no commercial objective. That's not operational efficiency — it's portfolio management applied to marketing. Technology fixes the symptom. Prioritization fixes the problem.

Revenue Marketing

Stop measuring marketing with marketing metrics

Impressions, reach and engagement tell me whether the content worked. They don't tell the CFO whether it was worth it. The question I use to evaluate any initiative is always the same: if I had to justify this investment to the board tomorrow, would I have the right numbers, or just the pretty ones? The difference between a Head of Marketing and a CMO isn't the title — it's which metric they choose to defend first in a revenue meeting.

AI in Marketing

AI doesn't fix a broken operating model

I did my postgraduate in AI applied to Marketing convinced technology would solve my scale problems. It solved some. But where the team had no defined process, AI accelerated the chaos instead of solving it — automating a badly prioritized request just delivers it faster. The lesson I carry: AI is a multiplier, not a substitute for strategy. Before asking which AI tool to implement, I always ask which business decision that tool will support. Without that answer, it's technology looking for a problem.
